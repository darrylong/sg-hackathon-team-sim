KEY FINDINGS
==============================================================================
- Training set has 30,000 tickets; P1 is the rarest priority (11.1%) yet the costliest to misroute - use class weights and check P1 recall.
- Teams are imbalanced: Server-HW 25.5% vs Billing-Ops 8.6%.
- Category alone does not decide the team: only 60.9% of Firmware tickets go to Server-HW.
- Product overrides the category default in 18 (category, product) pairs, e.g. Storage + ProLiant DL380 Gen11 -> Server-HW - the product must be a model feature.
- The portal severity tag agrees with the true priority only 44% of the time ('Critical' is really P1 just 36%) - strip it from the text or treat it as a weak feature.
- 212 severity tags contain typos, so any rule touching them must be fuzzy.
- Sentiment is a strong hint for priority: 28.8% of Angry tickets are P1 vs 2.7% of Positive ones - but most Angry tickets are still not P1, so tone alone is not enough.
- Chat is written differently: 58.9% of chat tickets are all lowercase vs 0.0% of emails - lowercase all text before vectorising.
- The shortest training ticket has 9 words, but 101 eval tickets have fewer than 8 - the model has never seen text that short, so these are prime candidates for abstaining.
- Curveball tickets test sarcasm, non-English text (Malay, German), all-caps shouting, vague 'it broke' messages, false urgency and out-of-scope requests (e.g. aircon); new products/channels vs training: none.
- Storage ranks #1 of 8 categories on average misroute cost (2.90): it is 13.1% of tickets but 26.8% of all P1s (P1 rate 22.6%).
- Storage is the most team-ambiguous category (65% to its top team): about 33% of Storage tickets go to Server-HW because they are about disks inside servers - the product decides it.
- Emotion is an early warning in Storage: an Angry Storage ticket is P1 45.8% of the time vs 5.7% for Positive; Storage ranks #1 of 8 on escalation risk (38.7%).
- Tone is tricky: 97 of 892 Storage P1 tickets use positive words ('great', 'love it', 'thanks for nothing') that are often sarcasm - a word-count model can be fooled.
